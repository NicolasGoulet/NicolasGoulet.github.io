## How to read this blog series?

This serie is an attempt at extracting value out of my maladive *penchant* for reading. It aims to serve as an entry point for some into classical litterature, for others, it may serve as takes from which to take inspiration or draw further reflections from. In any case, I will attempt to be as entertaining as possible, while always striving to remain informative. This means that while sections that will come across as pedantic will not be accompagnied with an exhaustive enough discussion to warrant such a tone. 

How can we even write about books nowadays, accounting for all different forms of readers? How can you satisfy the ones who majored in litterature, or even worse, those that pushed their studies even further in such fields? Among these, you might find sick individuals who will read the personal correspondance of authors, even try their hand at psychoanalyzing them. How to satisfy them while not losing well-meaning potential readers who are often spooked by the dignity we attribute to *classical* litterature ? The solution is an hybrid which I will try to offer. 

Another big reason for all these blog posts and blog series on this website (this being the first *How To Read* entry out of all my blogs), is that I just have no one to talk to about these things. Like, I am an adult-person and I have adult-person thoughts that deserve being uttered and shared (if I am to emulate other adult-persons, my peers). I have feelings and thoughts that I believe might come across as original, laughable, interesting, or even inspiring, or maybe all at once. I wish to convey that I do not take myself seriously, but I do take the things I will write about seriously.  The humor is meant to keep you attentive, dear reader.
