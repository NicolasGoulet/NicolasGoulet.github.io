## Intro

How do we help and guide the modern lambda reader towards such an old book? A book of which the context it was redacted in is so alien to us? In which, God forbid we open here this can of worms, even historical facts are subject of dispute among experts? ## (Re)Reading Great Books

I am often amazed by the incredible capacity of frontier LLMs, that have had what Stevan Harnad calls a *big gulp*, to remember with incredile memory where certain passages are from in any given book. It might often happen to me to try to look for a certain passage in a *quelconque* book, only to turn myself to ChatGPT and ask where certains ideas come up in *Illusions Perdues* whenever I need to cheer myself up. So while it has so thoroughly memorized the contents of every book, how come it hasn't published an unmeasurable amount of literary masterpieces ?

Dear reader, the act you are engaged in right now is the answer. This has become a *lieu commun* by now : even you rgrandma knows LLMs have no soul, no *human substance*, which is precisely what is required to create literary masterpieces. **Why should we read great books**? It is because art is not something that is projected unto us ; books, non-scientific ones at least, are not projectors, but rather an infinite amount of textures unto which we project ourselves. We will pick up this idea in a later blogpost where we will discuss what makes *the value of a movie*, if such a thing exists (it does and we will see how and why). For now, let's state the following : the value of an act such as investing hours upon hours to read printed words originally written centuries ago is **what do we make of it in our lives**, how that projection of ourselves unto the unique texture that is a piece of art allows has impacts on us, be they simply plesaure, distraction, or something more edifying. 

This previous argument to me always felt a bit like what is intended by Anglo-Saxon NPCs when they spout *auteur is dead* to seem more original than they actually are. I think this is a rather nuanced topic that deserves discussion here. First off, let's present what I'll label the *Interpret* position. When learning a piano piece, an act that like reading necessitates for most of us countless hours, you should start by listening to many different interpets while reading the score. While being true to yourself and what you hear in your heart, you often end up prefering one interpet over the other, or a collage of their interpretaions, guiding you towards how *you* as a pianist interpret the piece. Personally, this is often done alongside taking in the culture of the era it was composed in, and more precisely, what the composer took in. What was it that he liked reading? Or playing? Or listening? From all of this, you might get a general *domain* acrross which your interpretation may vary. 

So while this previous paragraph might feel like I subscribe to rather conservative views on how to interpret `the Arts` (imagine Contrapoints saying this line), there simply **are** some truths about more radical stances of *auteur is dead* : you **can** simply make up what you want of an art piece. And it is valid. And the act of interpretation and thinking about a piece of art is part of the piece of art. Let's give the spoilers to a future blogspot : the value of a piece of art is whatever we make of it in our lives. This is why there are no good or bad movies.  We will see in an upcoming blog series how these ideas can take use from Kant to Toby Fox.

Nothing written here is original, and all notions hinted at here are discussed better elsewhere by my betters. Also, yes in the beggining I insinuated that science books are projectors as if I would believe science is not a social construct. It was not adressed for style consideatriotns.

### Cervantes
