## Third Part

Now we are getting somewhere. This is the part where Don Quichottes gets quite serious, as I will soon demonstrate in the most academic of manners. ADDENDUM : I actually did not redact anything while I read this part, which makes my comments much poorer. Sorry!

## Highlight Hilarity

Although the introduction to this part of the blog was quite the serious one, it is the section that had me laughing the hardest. I waited too long before writing this and now most of my thoughts are gone. I will simply mention that no matter how bad things are getting, they seem to be constantly getting worse. The third part of this book contains humor around indigestions of various kinds, and speeches that left me speechleft. Don Quichottes takes what is literaly a bassin for barbers as a holy relic, and like many other jokes, they keep paying off. Really, this book is a masterpiece of humour and meta-narrative, that's kinda the gist of it. It is during this part the Don Quichottes gets his name, the Knight of the Sad Figure : I will leave to your pleasure to discover Sancho's reasonning that allowed him to come up with his name. 

While I would love to list exhaustively all these things that were hilarious, I want to highlight that Don Quichottes infamy gets even worse this time around : he wounds innoncents and **literally** frees prisoners for the sake of vibes. The dialogue he exchanges with the members of the law and the prisonners alike are uniquely delicious. I expect that sooner or later, the consequences of their actions will catch up to them. 

## Cardenio

Here the story finally *gets somewhere* : Don Quichottes seems to find what could be an actual quest. As our most pious Duo traverse desolate mountains in search of adventure (or to flee the authorities after having freed a dozen of criminals), they come accross what seem like an actually extraordinary sight for the first time, not a mere symptop of our Knight's folly. A man, running, jumping, going absolutely nuts, yet with a certain allure of dignity and nobility that catches the interest of our Knight. 

It is at this point that our couple start looking for the man, searching for traces of him, asking the few strangers they come accross in this wilderness if they had any information about him. That is when goat herders inform them that the man turns out to be quite dangerous, and that they'd better steer clear of him. These herders let them know that this man has decided to retire himself to the most wild place there be, to live out the rest of his days, due to causes unkown to them. While they try to help him and approach him to give him food, his folly - which fluctuates as one minute he would be sane, the other would go crazy - would sometime push him to take by force what they would offer him in the palm of their hands. Now, I have read many books. I have played many videogames. I know where story goes, and this is quite the old book. I may not have read as many chivalry books as Don Quichottes, but I know a quest to prove my valour when I see one : find the crazy man, bring him to his sense either by the wisdom of your words or the strenght of your will, freeing the herders from the risk of being attacked at any point by a crazed man. I could not foresee anything other than that from Don Quichottes. He would solve this problem. 

This is part of why this book is so incredible : all he actually cares about is knowing the story. Bro is actually like *wait a minute is there an actual interesting story there?* He literaly needs his fix of stories, and how can we not love him for that? Against the warning of the goat herders, he declares that he will litterally set out to find him and get to know his story ; but that his when, the man appears!

Cardenio, unlike everyone else in this book at this point, is a genuilely incredible and interesting individual. He doesn't feel like a real person and comes accross as otherwordly, straight out of a book, unlike most of the other side characters of this book so far. Cardenio and Quichottes exchanges are poignant. You feel like two fucking lunatics are finally vibing so hard because they are matched in their folly. They initially start discoursing quite well, and we get a quite satisfying story within a story, whereas I might have read a bit fast certain "stories" within the story offered to us thus far. While we are listening the Cardenio explaining his poignant backstory, that is when he makes the most incredible mistake : he mentions a chivalry book. 

I can't overstate how much of this feels like lunacy. Cardenio has stated so firmly that no one must interupt his story. Any sane man would have stayed mute : but our most real and most brave knight, is not like most men. At some point, Cardenio mentions that his lover left a note in a chivarly book - a most unimportant detail at this point - which is what causes Don Quichottes to, not unlike Cardenio, lose is sanity and contact with the real world, and start undertaking the most refined of tyrades on the quality of Ladies who have the *grandeur d'esprit* to have developped a taste for chivalry books. Of course, Cardenio starts loosing his mind a little and starts sliding in the direction of Don Quichottes, which litteraly makes the later go absolutely batshit insane. I will stop spoiling here ; Cardenio proceeds to kick everyone's ass. 

### Larping

So. Don Quichottes IS a larper. But how are we not all larpers? I would find suspicious whomever said they have nothing in common with our knight. Literaly after Cardenio beat their asses, Don Quichottes randomly decides to emulate certain of the most chivalrous knights that ever were by, like them (and Cardenio), *going crazy* --- let's insist on that --- out of sheer love desperation. He highlights, toothless as he is now, how he has had difficulties in emulating them by the exploits of his sword, and that he might as well, at this point, emulate them by how despaired they are in love affairs. 

He decides to find the wildest place there is, and to *start going crazy there*. I would love to once again spoil some of the humour, but it would be at the detrimend of you, my reader. I only invite you to think of what it would mean, for this man, **at this point**, to finally decide to go deliberaltey crazy. It is beyond hilarity. 

### New Challengers Appeared

To close out my vibes on the third part, I want to mention that I believe that the barber and the priest are somewhat analoguous to Quichottes and Sancho. While the later are out there actually fucking shit up and making things worse for everyone (Oh our knight! if only he knew!), the former couple seems more focused on righting his wrongs, on actually heping people.

You know, I regretted not writing down things as they came along. Let's mention that we have two mention of a young boy having pretty feet right at the start of this part. This is really nice. But then, they realize that they are actually the beautiful feet of not a young boy, but a young woman! This is really nice. So at this point, before going any further, I want to share a feeling I have. This makes me think of Delta Rune. The cross-dressing. The actually craziness of people. Cardenio, Cardenia, The Knight. Meta-Narrativeness. Am I saying that Toby Fox has something of the modern Cervantes? No because his life is too boring and he looks like a a cuck (UwU i love HIm bbIGg). As I said, turns out they were the feshowing 

During this part, our new protagonists form pacts and develop alliances of the most interesting kinds! Cardenio and Dorotea, back to their prime self, are now ready to set out to have their revegeance on the most vile Don Fernando, helped by the barber and priest. In parallel, Dorotea accepts to play the most pious of princess in hopes to convince our most pious knight to help in her quest, as he would never quit the station of his love, like the chivalrous knight he is. Of course, the exchanges betweeen Dorotea and Don Quichottes are hilarious, I need to bring to your attention how our Knight might be able of great logical reasoning. The priest, who loses his fake-beard in some comical context, stands up and glues it again to his, claiming it to be thanks to the virtue of some magic of his (or whatever). It is at this point that Don Quichottes mentions that such magic might have healing effects on the flesh, as wherever there was a beard ripped off a skin, there is bound to be wounded flesh. Therefore, it only stands to reason that such magic would have healing properties beyond simply gluing back a beard, and should be used as such. 

Oh our knight! A scientist before his time! Before we move on, the strongest part of this section often are simply the interactions between Sancho and Don Quichottes. Again, it is boring to point at something and say it is funny. The humour **is** the point often of this. Why would one take their time reading this book? My sister asked me again **why are you reading this? are you learning something**?

## Remarks on the Priest

I would like to share some thoughts on our priest. He is most knowledgeable about chivarly books ; he partakes in what he preaches to be but a waste of time. He has a very well-developped taste in such books, and it is reflected when he and the owner of an inn discuss precisely this subject. The owner, not knowing how to read but having the pleaasure of possessing a few books, relates how he and others around time of *moisson* get around the only one who knows to read, and they sit there, more than 30, transfigured hearing about various chivalrous exploits, that they believe to be true facts about historical events. 

Obviously, every characters in the scene note how this is similar to the folly of the Hidalgo, and kindly explain to the inn owner that the events described in these are not real and that they are simply written for the pleasures of *esprits oisifs* to what he answers *how could we allow books that lie so blatantly to be published with the seal of the Council of the King*, aka for the peasant (yes he is no bourgeois do not worry) the ultimate source of moral and intellectual authority. I just can't help but think as this sometimes as people reading manga. They then sift through other books until the priest litteraly finds a manuscript that he wants to read, and we have all our characters stand around to hear the story, which I haven't read yet, wanting to write here. 

I can easily see myself arguing very convingly in that same manner around *les malfaits* of playing video games, watching TV, reading mangas etc, to then turn around, sift through someones's collection of whatever of the above and discuss their merits. I want to bring it up again because, just like in this book, I paused re-playing Deltarune Chapter 3 because I was about to be engaged in a meta-narrative (a story inside a story) where our characters literaly sit on a couch and a play a game (I really want to write about this in replaying!!) and it is one the best feeling ever.

However, and I am not very knowledgeable in this, and by the start of the Second Book I will know more, it feels to me unfathomably avant-garde the levels of irony and meta-narrativeness and meta-commentary that is present in this book. There are so many levels of appreciation. Let's just bring up a random fact I forgot to mention. There are many conspiracy theories about who wrote the fake part 2. So turns out that one of the criminals that Quichottes breaks free earlier *might* be inspired from a real person, and that some person believe that this individual felt insulted that he was described exhibiting such infamy, that he decided to write the fake second book. Like what. 

The more interesting point I think, is that I am maybe now getting why the more smarterer person than me might say that this is a great book (because let's not lie I read the whole introduction and everything and everyone is saying it is the greatest thing ever in the commentary but it just feels like they are not explaining clearly why) because it is very avant-garde about the meta-narrative stuff? As I shall soon write, most of the fourth part feels dedicated to meta-narratives (stories within stories).

## Stories Within Stories

While there had already been sufficient meta aspects to this book, when Don Quichottes and his troupe returns to the famous Inn where so much happened in the first parts, the book kinda stop dead in its tracks, and we are treated to a whole cavalcade of secondary characters, of stories within stories. During these segments, Cervantes writes masterfully : we, as readers, are very much like these described characters. They all seem obsessed by the idea of finding good stories, of learning life histories of others etc. There is something so profundly humans in being bored and seeking alleviation from that boredom through books, and if they enlighten us (*nous élève* in French, raises us toward something), that is even better! And I won't lie, some of these segments, these stories within stories really really bored me at times.

But there is still something so modern about these takes. Remember, at that time, it was suspicious to be wasting your time with leasurly readings : while I am failing to conjure the right words in this instant, it feels to me like there is a dimensionality where you can find these early reactions to *books meant to be read for pleasure* and people discarding the artistic merits of certain video games. And so, this existantial boredom that we all feel, on which so much has been written, was already plaguing these people centuries ago! Humans love a good story, we are evolutionarily attuned for it, and I will talk in another blog post on the evolutionary origins of narratives.
